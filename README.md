# What Takes Hold

After a fire, Eva drafts a possible homecoming: a Sunday lunch at which everyone has found a place. The account makes returning imaginable. It circulates, is corrected, is copied again. A sentence repeated often enough begins to pass for agreement. While Eva negotiates the distance between a workable home and a life she wants, Ruth plans her garden and puts aside half her supper. Borrowed sentences spread through the book with the ease of suggested replies, quietly occupying the spaces between people. *What Takes Hold* is a novel of provisional arrangements and irreversible change, of the words used to make a future habitable, and what grows, almost unnoticed, beyond the reach of revision.
