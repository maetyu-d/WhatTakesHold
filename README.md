# What Takes Hold

After a fire, Eva describes a possible homecoming: a Sunday lunch at which everyone has found a place. The account makes returning imaginable. It circulates, is corrected, is copied again. A sentence repeated often enough begins to pass for agreement. While Eva negotiates the space between the practicalities of home and a life she wants, and Ruth plans her garden and puts aside half her supper, borrowed sentences spread with the ease of suggested replies, quietly occupying the distance between people. *What Takes Hold* is an experimental novella of provisional arrangements and irreversible change, of the words used to make a future habitable and palatable, and what grows, almost unnoticed, out of reach.
